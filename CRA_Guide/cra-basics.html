What the CRA is

Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, the Cyber Resilience Act, sets horizontal cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements. It works like the familiar CE regimes (LVD, EMC, RED), but for cybersecurity.

The CRA in one paragraph

The CRA is a product law, not an operator law. To place a product with digital elements on the EU market, the manufacturer has to:

Design

Meet the essential requirements

Design to Annex I Part I (secure by default, secure updates, access control, integrity…). Requirements →

Maintain

Handle vulnerabilities

Run a vulnerability-handling process for the whole support period (Annex I Part II): SBOM, CVD policy, free security updates.

Report

Report exploited vulnerabilities

Report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to ENISA/CSIRT within 24 h (Art. 14). Reporting →

Declare

Document, declare, mark

Keep technical documentation (Annex VII), sign the EU declaration of conformity and affix the CE mark.

The rules apply no matter where the manufacturer is based (EU, India, USA). What triggers them is making the product available on the EU market.

Engineering partner's positionA design-services company that develops a product for a client is usually not the manufacturer. The client's legal obligations become the partner's engineering deliverables. The partner becomes the manufacturer only for products, SDKs or apps it sells under its own name.

Is the CRA a paid or restricted document?

No. The regulation itself is public EU law and free to read. What costs money are the technical standards used to show compliance.
