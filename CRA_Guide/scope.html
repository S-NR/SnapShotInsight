Scope & exclusions

The CRA covers any product with digital elements: hardware or software, including its remote data processing and separately sold components, whose intended or reasonably foreseeable use involves a direct or indirect, logical or physical data connection to a device or network (Art. 2–3).

What that means in practice

Almost any product with firmware and an interface is in scope.

Typical productIn scope?Why
Industrial sensor/transmitter with Modbus RS485, USB, EthernetYesPhysical/logical data connection
Device whose only "interface" is an SD card or a service portYes, very likelyAn indirect/physical data connection still counts
IoT device + cloud backend + mobile appYes, all threeThe cloud counts as remote data processing when the device needs it to perform a function
PC configuration tool sold with/for a productYesSoftware product with digital elements, also when free with the device
Pure SaaS with no product attachedNo (generally)Not a product. NIS2 or other law may apply
Free/open-source software developed non-commerciallyNoMonetised or commercially supported FOSS is in scope; open-source stewards have a light regime
Identical spare partsNoArt. 2(6)

Sector exclusions (Art. 2(2)–(7))

These sectors already regulate cybersecurity in their own law, so the CRA does not apply.

SectorGoverning law instead
Medical devices / in-vitro diagnosticsReg. (EU) 2017/745 (MDR), 2017/746 (IVDR)
Type-approved motor vehicles & trailersReg. (EU) 2019/2144 (in practice UNECE R155/R156)
Certified civil aviation equipmentReg. (EU) 2018/1139
Marine equipmentDirective 2014/90/EU
National security / defence / classified dataExcluded outright
Other sectors with equal or higher protectionOnly via a Commission delegated act (Art. 2(5))
Watch the edgesA lab instrument that is not a medical device is in scope. Test-bench equipment for automotive labs is in scope; only components of a type-approved vehicle are excluded.

Quick scope checker

1. Does the product contain firmware or software?

2. Does it have any data connection (bus, USB, SD card, radio, Ethernet, service/debug port used in the field)?

3. Is it made available on the EU market in a commercial context (sold, or supplied free with a paid product)?

4. Is it a medical/IVD device, part of a type-approved vehicle, certified aviation, marine equipment, or defence-only?

5. Is its core function one of: router/modem/switch, network interface, OS, boot manager, VPN, firewall/IDS, smart-home security, smart meter gateway, secure element/HSM, security-function MCU?

Answer the questions to see the result.
