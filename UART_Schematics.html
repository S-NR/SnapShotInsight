UART Pin Layout Schematics

Ensure both sides share common GND and match IO voltage (3.3V vs 5V). Add series resistors (22–100Ω) if desired, and decoupling caps on VCC.

1) MCU ↔ USB-TTL Adapter (FT232 / CP2102) — Async

Wiring summary: MCU TX → Adapter RX, MCU RX ← Adapter TX, common GND. Set adapter VCC to match MCU (3.3V recommended).

Minimal BOM: USB-TTL adapter, optional 22–100Ω series resistors, 0.1µF decoupling cap.

2) MCU ↔ MAX232 ↔ DB9 (RS-232)

Wiring summary: MCU TX↔MAX232 TTL pin ↔ MAX232 RS232 pin ↔ DB9. MAX232 performs voltage inversion and level shifting. Add recommended caps.

Minimal BOM: MAX232 (or equivalent), 4 × 1µF caps (or per datasheet), DB9 connector, decoupling cap.

3) MCU ↔ RS-485 Transceiver (Half-Duplex)

Wiring summary: DI/RO pins map to MCU TX/RX; control DE to enable transmitter (assert DE before sending). Tie RE appropriately for receive mode. Add termination and biasing resistors on bus.

Minimal BOM: RS485 transceiver IC, 120Ω terminator(s), pull resistors for bias, decoupling cap, TVS optional.

4) Synchronous UART — MCU (Master) ↔ MCU (Slave) with CK

Wiring summary: Cross TX/RX as usual and connect CK from master to slave. Ensure both use same IO voltage and configure master/slave roles in USART peripheral settings.

Minimal BOM: Two dev boards or MCUs, optional series resistors, decoupling caps. Use scope or logic analyzer to verify clock & data timing.

Additional Notes & Quick Checklist

  • Always tie grounds between devices.
  • Match VCC levels or use proper level translators.
  • Use series resistors (22–100Ω) to protect against contention and reduce ringing.
  • Place 0.1µF close to each IC VCC pin for decoupling.
  • Use a logic analyzer when debugging: it shows TTL levels and (for sync) the CK alignment.
