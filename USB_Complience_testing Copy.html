USB Eye Diagram & I/J/K Compliance Test — Complete Beginner Guide

This document explains the Eye Diagram and USB Compliance Mask Testing (I/J/K) in a simple and visual way. Even if you have zero prior knowledge of signal integrity or high-speed USB, you will understand:

  • What is an Eye Diagram?
  • What are I/J/K mask tests?
  • How to interpret pass and fail results
  • How the waveform should look in a correct USB device
USB compliance testing ensures signal quality so devices can communicate reliably with any host or hub without bit errors.

1. What is an Eye Diagram?

An Eye Diagram is created by repeatedly overlaying thousands of digital signal transitions over time. The result visually resembles a human eye — hence the name.

It shows:

  • Signal amplitude
  • Noise margin (vertical opening)
  • Timing margin (jitter) (horizontal opening)
  • Rise & fall time
The bigger the opening of the eye, the healthier the high-speed signal. A closed eye indicates distortion, noise, reflections, and jitter.

Understanding the Two Waveforms

  • Vertical Opening = voltage margin (noise immunity)
  • Horizontal Opening = timing margin (jitter tolerance)
  • Crossing Point should be centered and balanced
  • If mask boundary is touched → FAIL
If the eye collapses at the intersection, it means excessive jitter, reflections, bandwidth limitation, or cable quality issues.

3. What is USB I / J / K Compliance Mask Test?

USB compliance uses three mask patterns — I, J, K — applied to the center of the Eye Diagram. If the signal ever enters the mask region, it fails.

  • I mask: checks Inter-packet idle/sync region
  • J mask: checks steady-state logic high
  • K mask: checks steady-state logic low
Mask testing determines whether the actual signal waveform stays outside the forbidden zone.

4. Pass / Fail Criteria

  • PASS: waveform stays completely outside mask region
  • FAIL: waveform touches or crosses mask

5. Common Reasons for Failures

  • Poor cable or connector quality
  • Reflections due to impedance mismatch
  • Power noise or ground instability
  • PCB routing issues (length mismatch / via stubs)
  • Slow rise or fall time
