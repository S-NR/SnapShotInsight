🔧 What is MTBF?

MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures) is a reliability metric that predicts the average time a component or system operates before failing.

Formula:

MTBF = Total Operating Time / Number of Failures
  • Expressed in hours (e.g., 100,000 hrs)
  • Assumes random, repairable failures (not due to wear-out)

📊 Example Interpretation

If a power supply has an MTBF of 200,000 hours:

  • It does not mean it will last 200,000 hours.
  • It means: In 1,000 units, 5 may fail per 1,000 hours.

⚙️ MTBF Calculation – Detailed

1. Empirical MTBF (observed data)

MTBF = Total Run Time of All Units / Failures Observed

Example: 100 devices × 1000 hours, 2 failed ⇒ MTBF = 50,000 hrs

2. Predicted MTBF (standards-based)

Engineers use standards like:

  • MIL-HDBK-217F – Military electronic systems
  • Telcordia SR-332 – Telecom/commercial equipment
  • FIDES – European aerospace/defense

MTBF = 1 / λ_total where λ = failure rate

MYTH: “My PSU has 500,000-hour MTBF, so it will last 50 years.”
FACT: MTBF is a statistical average, not a guarantee.

✅ How to Use MTBF in Design

  • Compare vendor reliability before choosing components.
  • Use redundancy for critical systems.
  • Include watchdog timers and self-diagnostics.
  • Derate components to reduce stress (e.g. 60% of rated voltage).
  • Apply proper thermal management to prevent accelerated aging.
